# Rig arms, first run: gp3-capped disk and sparse tenants (2026-10-31)

First measurements from the opt-in arms added for expert review 2026-10-29 (runbook:
[`../runbooks/rig-arms.md`](../runbooks/rig-arms.md)). Commit under test: `e5f45291` (main after the
Wave 1 merge). Rig: 5 nodes on a 12 vCPU / 94 GB colima VM, replication ON, `SHARD_FLUSH_INTERVAL_MS=30000`,
1 GB `vm.dirty_background_bytes` unless stated, elixir driver in the compose network, a fresh
`colima restart` before every `up`. `clock lags the store` was 0 in every cell. Logs are not committed.
The auth arm (#18) has NOT been run.

## 1. Sparse tenants: `POOL_DRAIN_GRACE_MS` 250 vs longer (review #2) - a large, repeatable effect

`./chaos.sh tpc-sparse 512 120 1000 <grace,grace>`: 512 tenants, one open-loop one-shot write per tenant
per second (about 512 ops/s fleet-wide), 120 ops per tenant, two rounds per `up`. Replicas and data on the
same disk, no cap. Two fresh `up`s per grace.

| grace (ms) | up | round | p50 ms | p95 ms | p99 ms | pool hit% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 250 (shipped default) | 1 | 1 | 8.7 | 15.4 | 420 | 0.9 |
| 250 | 1 | 2 | 39 | 3,551 | 3,992 | 37.5 |
| 250 | 2 | 1 | 8.3 | 14.5 | 440 | 0.9 |
| 250 | 2 | 2 | 13.6 | 2,920 | 3,382 | 25.0 |
| 5000 | 1 | 1 | 1.7 | 3.5 | 6.1 | 95.4 |
| 5000 | 1 | 2 | 1.6 | 3.5 | 6.1 | 95.3 |
| 5000 | 2 | 1 | 1.6 | 3.5 | 6.0 | 95.3 |
| 5000 | 2 | 2 | 1.7 | 3.4 | 6.0 | 95.4 |
| 1500 / 1100 (alternating, one `up`) | 3 | 1-4 | 1.7 | 3.4 | 6.0-6.2 | 95.4-95.5 |

- Every 250 round is slow; every 5000/1500/1100 round is flat at about 6 ms p99. Round 2 of a 250 `up` is
  about 10x worse than its round 1 (p99 3.4-4.0 s vs 0.42-0.44 s), in both ups. Within a 5000 `up` there is
  no drift.
- The benefit is a function of tenant inter-arrival against the grace: a 1,100 ms grace is as good as 5,000 ms
  for tenants that write once a second. A tenant that returns after the grace expires pays the full cost again.
- Mechanism evidence (one 250 `up`, `fathom_replication_commit_stage_duration`): the 250 rounds are a reset
  storm, about 60,500 `reset="true"` commits of 62,464 in round 1 (one new WAL generation per gap), and in
  the slow round the reset commit's collect stage had mean 164 ms, p99 <= 5,000 ms, against mean 8 ms in round 1.
  At grace 5000 resets fall to about 2,200 per round. So the cost is the close-time checkpoint plus the
  follower's reset absorb (review #5, expert review 2026-10-26 #18), and it GROWS over the life of the `up`
  when every gap resets. The drift mechanism itself is not isolated; it is consistent with the absorb's
  fsyncs queueing behind writeback, but that was not tested.
- Memory and fd cost, measured once per grace (1,024 tenants, one write per tenant per second, ~1,000 ops/s
  fleet-wide, fresh `up`, one round, 5 nodes sampled every 5 s, mid-round values; a throwaway sampler (not committed),
  BEAM figures from `:erlang.memory(:total)` and `/proc/self/fd` via rpc, RSS from `docker stats`):

  | grace (ms) | round p99 | BEAM total, fleet | open fds, fleet | container RSS, fleet | pool hit% |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 250 | 674 ms | ~825 MiB | ~6,900 | ~1,770 MiB | 0.3 |
  | 5000 | 43 ms | ~917 MiB | ~8,640 | ~1,773 MiB | 94.9 |

  The extra cost of holding handles at this scale is about +95 MiB BEAM memory (+12%) and about +1,700 fds
  (+25%, roughly 1.7 fds per held tenant, ~340 per node), with container RSS unchanged within noise (the
  SQLite C-heap and page cache are in RSS, not in the BEAM figure). Single runs; the BEAM and fd deltas are
  large against the sample-to-sample spread (about +-20 MiB and +-150 fds late in a round; the 250 round's fd count was still settling down from ~7,700 to ~6,800), the RSS delta is not distinguishable
  from zero. The held-handle count is bounded by tenants active within the grace, at most `2 x max_per_scope`
  per shard, so the cost scales with tenants active per grace window, not with grace alone. The fd cost is the
  one that matters: it lands on `emfile` and on the pool fd budget that `check_pool_fd_budget!` warns about
  (`lib/fathom/application.ex`), and a fleet sized for the 250 ms world holds about a quarter more fds at 5 s.
  Not measured: larger fleets, higher per-tenant rates, or a grace between 250 and 5,000 (1,100 and 1,500 had
  the same p99 as 5,000 at 512 tenants, so the smaller grace should hold fewer handles at the same benefit
  for one-second tenants).
- Changing the shipped default is NOT done: review #2 marks it Jepsen-gated (it moves the close-time
  checkpoint, the "idle => last close unlinked the WAL" invariant, and flush-barrier timing) and it has a
  memory trade that is the maintainer's call.

## 2. gp3-capped disk (review #16): the cap binds, but the low-rate ladder is confounded by section 1

`RIG_DISK_CAP=gp3`: 3,000 IOPS and 125 MiB/s per node on the device behind `/var/lib/docker`; `disk-cap
probe` read 126 MiB/s and 3,084-3,089 IOPS before every run. Open loop, `tpc-tail 1024 <txns> 1000`.

| arm | 3,000 txn/s | 3,600 txn/s |
|---|---|---|
| uncapped control | p99_excl 123, 138, 148 ms | 9.9 s, 1 run, saturated |
| gp3, one shared cap | 32.2 s, 39.7 s, 43.4 s | 82 s, 82 s, 69 s |
| gp3-split (data and replica device capped separately) | 3.5 s, 2.7 s, 36.0 s | 43 s, 23 s, 8.0 s |

- At the reference rate the capped arms are over capacity (p99 tens of seconds against ~130 ms uncapped).
  This confirms the cap binds at 600 txn/s per node. It does not say where the knee is.
- The lower ladder (1,000-2,500 txn/s, one run per rate, 150-200 txns per tenant) does not locate the knee:
  the UNCAPPED control is also bad there (p99_excl 3.0 s at 1,000 and 3.2 s at 1,500, with ~20-90 ms windows
  for the first 60-100 s and ~3 s windows after), against 123-148 ms at 3,000. At 1,000 txn/s each tenant
  writes about once a second, which is the section 1 regime (gaps over 250 ms), so those cells measure the
  sparse-tenant path, not the cap. The capped ladder points (2.9 s at 1,000, 1.9 s at 1,500) are inside the
  same confound.
- IO accounting (VM `/proc/diskstats`, all nodes, both disks): about 3.6 write IOs and 132 KB per transaction
  including seeding. At 1,000 txn/s that is about 660 IOPS and 26 MB/s per node, far below the cap, so the
  cap was not the binding constraint at 1,000-1,500.
- One capped run with `vm.dirty_background_bytes` 128 MB gave p99_excl 0.76 s at 1,000 and 1.25 s at 1,500 txn/s
  against 2.9 s and 1.9 s at the 1 GB default. Single runs, inside the confound above; not evidence.
- Not done: a ladder of the capped arms at the SAME inter-arrival as the 3,000 txn/s reference but with a
  grace that removes the sparse confound (for example `POOL_DRAIN_GRACE_MS=5000`), which is what would locate
  the capped knee. Repeats of the capped cells beyond one pass.

## What this changes in the audit

- #2 (sparse tenants): confirmed on Linux/ext4 with replication, and by a margin far larger than any other
  finding in the audit. It is a p99 lever for sparse populations, and every rig p99 in `docs/reviews/` was
  taken on a population that never idles, so none of them can show it.
- #16 (capped disk): the cap binds at the reference rate; the capacity below it is not yet measured.
- #5 (reset absorb fsync) and expert review 2026-10-26 #18 gain a measured reason: resets are the cost when
  gaps exceed the grace.
