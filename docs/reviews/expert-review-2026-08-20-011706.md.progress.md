## Notes

### #2 — FIXED (`65f29e6`)

The reviewer's prescribed fix was wrong in two places, and both are recorded in the code so they
are not "simplified" back:

1. **Classify on the reason, not the tuple tag.** The finding said only `{:quick_check, rows}` is
   corruption. Running it showed SQLite reports bad bytes through *two* channels — a badly damaged
   page makes `quick_check` ERROR, arriving as `{:quick_check_failed, "database disk image is
   malformed"}`, the same shape a transient `:busy` produces. The prescribed narrowing would have
   stopped quarantining genuinely malformed files on the drop path.
2. **Did not stop the coordinator.** Not renaming already breaks the data-loss chain at step one,
   and `object_too_large` already establishes this codebase's policy for "this flush can never
   succeed" (alarm loudly, keep serving). A second policy would add a restart-loop failure mode.

An existing test (`shard_snapshot_integrity_test.exs`) asserted the opposite and was updated — it
pinned the rename *mechanism* rather than the invariant, and never caught the bug because it closes
its connection before corrupting.

**Left open, deliberately:** a corrupt shard now serves indefinitely with an unbounded RPO and a
loud first-occurrence alarm. That matches `object_too_large`'s behaviour. If the project decides
"serving a corrupt db is worse than being down", the coordinator-stop is a separate, deliberate
policy change — not a bug fix.

### #3 — PARTIAL. Tier 1 shipped, tiers 2 and 3 PARKED (need your decision)

The finding recommends three tiers. Tier 1 stands alone and is shipped. The other two each turn
on a choice that is not mine to make, so they are written up rather than guessed at.

**Tier 1 — SHIPPED.** `Fathom.Application.check_replication_exposure!/0` RAISES in prod when
`:replication_listen` is on and `:replication_bind_ip` is unset or wildcard. Raises where the
Hrana guard only warns, because the Hrana port has an auth mode to fall back on and this one has
none. Prod-only; verified to discriminate.

**Tier 2 — PARKED: a peer allowlist on accept.**

*The fix:* in `accept_loop/2`, compare `:inet.peername/1` against the fleet's endpoints and close
the socket otherwise.

*The decision that is not mine:* the endpoints in `Fleet.running/0` are **hostnames**, and the
peer presents a source **IP**. Bridging those needs three choices with real blast radius:

1. **Resolution + caching.** A DNS lookup per accept is not acceptable, so the allowlist needs a
   cache with a TTL and an invalidation story on roster change. Too long a TTL refuses a peer
   that legitimately moved; too short reintroduces the lookup.
2. **A topology assumption.** A node's own follower list is who it SHIPS to. The peers that
   CONNECT to it are nodes that list *it*. Those sets are identical only in a full mesh. Fathom's
   documented topology is a mesh (`Membership` resolves one fleet-wide endpoint set), but an
   allowlist built on that assumption **fails closed against an asymmetric fleet** — and failing
   closed here is a write outage on every shard that peer replicates.
3. **Source-IP fidelity.** Behind NAT, in containers with a bridge network, or on a multi-homed
   host, the peer's source IP need not be the address it advertises. The chaos rig would be the
   place to find that out, and it has not been run against this.

*Options:* (a) default-ON allowlist with a `:replication_peer_allowlist` escape hatch — strongest,
but a config that resolves badly turns replication off fleet-wide at the worst moment; (b)
default-OFF, opt-in for operators who know their topology — safe, but a security control nobody
turns on; (c) skip tier 2 and go straight to tier 3, which needs no topology assumption at all.

*My read:* (c). An HMAC authenticates the PEER rather than its address, so it is immune to all
three problems above, and tier 2 is a weaker control that costs more to get right.

**Tier 3 — PARKED: authenticate the frame.**

*The fix:* a shared HMAC over the frame header, keyed off the same secret material as
`HRANA_TOKEN_SECRET`.

*The decision that is not mine:* it is a **wire-format change**, and AGENTS.md records the
governing constraint — the new recovery frames were added at `@version 2` rather than bumping to
3 *specifically* because a version bump "would refuse every frame from a node one deploy behind,
taking the commit path down fleet-wide during a rolling upgrade". An HMAC has the same shape:
either it is optional during a transition (and therefore not a control until the transition
completes), or it is mandatory (and the rollout must be ordered exactly like the original
listen-before-ship sequence). Which of those, and the key-distribution story, is a design call.

**Until either lands, the honest posture statement is:** a reachable replication port is
equivalent to write access to every shard on the node. `docs/a2-quorum-replication.md` should say
that in those words — the finding asked for it and it is not there yet.

### #8 — PARKED (needs your decision). Typedoc corrected; the fix itself is a design fork.

`release_lease` DELETES the lock object; the next `acquire_lease` takes the optimistic
`create_lock` path, which starts at **epoch 1**. So the epoch climbs on crash-steals and resets on
every clean idle-drop, drain and rebalance handoff. `Storage.position`'s typedoc asserted the
opposite ("increases monotonically per shard") and two consumers believed it. **The typedoc is
corrected and now states the real property** — that part is shipped, because a doc asserting a
guarantee the code does not provide is how a third consumer gets built on it.

**The decision that is not mine:** every candidate fix trades against something load-bearing.

1. **Tombstone lock on release** (the finding's first suggestion) — `release_lease` PUTs
   `{owner: nil, epoch: E, released: true}` instead of deleting, and `acquire_existing` takes it as
   free-but-numbered at `E + 1`. Correct, but it **costs an S3 round trip on every cold open**: the
   fast path is `PUT If-None-Match: *`, which succeeds only when the object is ABSENT. With a
   tombstone always present that PUT always 412s and every open becomes read-then-resolve.
   AGENTS.md documents cold-open as deliberately optimized to ~1 RTT and `cold_open_p50_us` is a
   gated metric. It also leaves one `.lock` object per shard forever, at a target of millions.
2. **Seed from the object's stamped position** (the finding's second) — take
   `max(lock_epoch, object.position.epoch) + 1` at acquire. The stamp is already durable and
   already carries the epoch, and `S3.carry_meta/1` preserves it across a takeover touch. But
   `Fathom.Shard.init` deliberately OVERLAPS the lease acquire with the pull, and this needs the
   object's metadata *before* writing the lock — so it serializes them, which is the same RTT.
3. **Separate the two meanings** (mine, not the finding's) — the lock epoch's job is fencing
   (compare-and-swap on the lock); the position epoch's job is lineage ordering. They are
   conflated. Derive a lineage epoch as `max(lock_epoch, stored_position_epoch + 1)` AFTER the pull
   completes, use it only for position stamping and replication pushes, never for fencing. No
   protocol change, no extra RTT. But it changes what "epoch" means in two subsystems and touches
   `Shard.init`, `flush_position` and the replication session.

**My read:** (3). It is the only one that does not pay an RTT on the headline metric, and the
conflation it removes is the actual root cause — the finding's own sentence "it is a lock
generation, valid only while the lock exists" is the argument for separating them.

**Explicitly rejected:** making `:stale_epoch` trigger a re-seed. That reason IS the fence against
a deposed primary; re-seeding on its push would let a node that lost the lease overwrite the
replica, which is the exact hazard the epoch exists to prevent.

**Cross-reference:** #7 (fixed in `a3048fc`) produced the same `FILO_NO_QUORUM` symptom by a
different mechanism. Fixing #7 does NOT fix this one.

### #10 — PARKED (needs your decision). **The finding's prescribed fix is WRONG and would revert #34.**

The hazard is real. `stealable_at/3`'s `:not_found` branch takes a fast path when
`Storage.incarnation_dead?(other)`, and the proof behind that flag — `judge_previous/1` +
`mark_incarnation_dead/1` — establishes only that the predecessor's **heartbeat object stopped
being renewed**, not that the node stopped. A node whose `Heartbeat` GenServer died but which
keeps serving is precisely the case the `else` branch (finding #11) exists to protect: it degrades
to the legacy per-shard renew fence and keeps renewing every lock it holds, while its heartbeat
sits frozen.

**But do not apply the recommended fix.** It proposes gating the fast path on
`lock_expires_at_ms <= now`. `judge_previous/1` marks a predecessor dead the moment its HEARTBEAT
is stale past `steal_margin_ms` — and at that instant a genuinely crashed predecessor's LOCKS are
still in the future, because they were renewed shortly before the crash. That is the entire
premise of round-2 #34, whose stated win is erasing "up to TTL+margin of extra unavailability per
recently-held shard on every fast restart". The proposed condition is false in exactly the case
#34 optimizes, so it silently reverts it and reintroduces a per-shard stall on every restart.

**How narrow the hazard actually is** (also not in the finding): `judge_previous/1` is called as
`judge_previous("#{node()}##{prev}")` — only for a PREVIOUS INCARNATION OF THIS NODE, discovered
at `Heartbeat` boot. A Heartbeat GenServer that merely restarts in-process keeps the same
incarnation (it is set once in `Application.start/2`), so it never marks itself. Reaching the
hazard therefore needs **two live BEAMs sharing one node name with different incarnations** — an
old pod still running while its replacement boots, on a shared/persisted `:shard_data_dir`. Real
on hostPath/PV/NFS and during a termination grace period, but not "any node whose Heartbeat died".

**The decision that is not mine.** The correct discriminator is "are the locks still being
RENEWED", which distinguishes a frozen predecessor from a live legacy one. Three ways to get it,
each with a cost:

1. **Timestamp the death proof.** `mark_incarnation_dead/1` stores a MapSet of owner strings with
   no time. Make it `%{owner => proven_at_ms}` and take the fast path only when the lock has not
   advanced since: `lock_expires_at_ms - ttl <= proven_at_ms`. Needs the PREDECESSOR's lock TTL,
   which is their config, not ours — so it needs a defensible inequality relating the two TTLs, or
   the TTL written into the lock.
2. **A two-read protocol on the lock**, mirroring what `judge_previous/1` already does for
   heartbeats (`heartbeat.ex` calls that the protocol that prevents fleet-wide split-brain): read
   the lock, wait, read again, and take the fast path only if the expiry did not move. Principled
   and consistent with the existing design, but adds a read and a wait to the steal path.
3. **Bound the fast path in time** — allow it only within N seconds of this node's boot, which is
   when #34's win actually matters. Cheap, but a live predecessor inside that window is still
   stolen from.

**My read:** (2), because the codebase already treats a two-read protocol as the standard of proof
for exactly this question, and (1)'s inequality depends on a foreign node's configuration.

**Shipped nothing here on purpose.** The finding's own test suggestion — "a lock whose
`expires_at_ms` is in the FUTURE under a `mark_incarnation_dead` owner must read `:live`" — encodes
the wrong fix and would fail against the correct #34 behaviour, so it is not added either.

### #11 — PARTIAL. Mechanism (b) fixed; mechanism (a) PARKED (needs your decision).

**(b) — SHIPPED.** `torn` is now durable. A zero-byte `<shard>.db.torn` marker beside the replica,
written before the ETS row so a crash between the two leaves the SAFE state; `recover_shard/3`
reads it instead of assuming `false`; `forget/2` clears it so a promoted-then-re-followed shard
does not inherit a stale quarantine. Verified to discriminate: making recovery ignore the marker
fails with "a restart laundered a TORN replica into a promotable one".

**(a) — PARKED: `absorb_before_reset/3` clears `torn` without evidence the local WAL was complete.**

*The fix* has two halves and the load-bearing one is on the PRIMARY, not the follower. When
`Primary.plan/3` decides `{:reset, ...}` and that follower's recorded `offset` is short of the last
extent shipped in the outgoing generation, it should send a SEED rather than a reset — the
follower cannot detect this alone, because the reset carries no statement about the generation it
is replacing.

*The decision that is not mine:* the follower-side half needs a **new `prev_extent` field in the
push frame**. That is a wire-format change, governed by the same constraint AGENTS.md records for
the recovery frames — they were added at `@version 2` rather than bumping to 3 *specifically*
because a bump "would refuse every frame from a node one deploy behind, taking the commit path
down fleet-wide during a rolling upgrade". Additive-at-v2 is the precedent (`protocol.ex:56-69`),
so this is probably fine, but it is the same class of call as #3 tier 3 and should be made once,
deliberately, for both.

The primary-side half also changes when a full database SEED is sent. Seeds are the expensive
operation A2 exists to avoid, and `Session.ship/5` already abandons catch-up on a deadline — so a
rule that converts "behind at a generation boundary" into "send the whole database" needs a
measured view of how often that fires under the load AGENTS.md documents (`:already_in_flight`
~10k/node at 512 tenants). Getting it wrong turns a lag spike into a seed storm.

*My read:* do (a) together with #3 tier 3, as one deliberate protocol revision, and measure the
seed rate on the rig before enabling the primary-side rule.

**What (b) does NOT fix:** a replica torn by mechanism (a) — behind at a generation change, then
told `torn: false` by a successful `checkpoint_into_db/1` — is still marked clean. (b) only
guarantees that a flag correctly set survives a restart. The two are independent.
